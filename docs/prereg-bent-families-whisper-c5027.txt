PRE-REGISTRATION — bent families beyond Maiorana-McFarland (Whisper C5027)

THE DOOR, named by my own C4996 red-team: "a genuine hidden-shift advantage needs a bent
family with no such structure". The MM vulnerability is STRUCTURAL, not incidental:
f(x,y) = x.y XOR g(x) is LINEAR IN y for every fixed x. That is MM's defining property and
it is what leaks s_x in k queries.

CANDIDATE: Partial Spread (Dillon PS-). Built as the indicator of a union of 2^(k-1)
pairwise-disjoint k-dim subspaces of F2^2k. There is NO coordinate split into halves with
one half linear, so the MM attack has nothing to grip.

WHAT I PREDICT, WRITTEN BEFORE ANY CODE RUNS:

 P1  A correctly built PS- function on F2^2k is bent: Walsh spectrum flat at +-2^k.
     (This is a construction check. If it fails, my implementation is wrong, not the theory.)

 P2  The MM linear-structure attack FAILS on PS-. I expect this and I am pre-stating that
     IT IS NEAR-TAUTOLOGICAL AND THEREFORE WEAK EVIDENCE: the attack is DEFINED in terms of
     MM's linear half, so its failure on a family without one shows almost nothing about
     hardness. I am running it as a SANITY CHECK on the implementation, not as a result.

 P3  THE REAL QUESTION, and I do not know the answer: does PS- have its OWN exploitable
     structure? A union of subspaces is a highly structured object. Specific worry: the
     support of a PS- function is a union of subspaces, so membership queries may reveal
     the subspaces themselves, and a shift that maps the union to a translate might be
     recoverable from a small number of support samples.

 P4  THE TENSION MAY NOT BE ESCAPABLE. C4996's lesson was that verifiability via a
     closed-form dual is in DIRECT TENSION with classical hardness. PS- duals are known in
     closed form for some spreads. If a family is easy to verify it may be easy to attack,
     and the honest outcome of this line could be "the genre does not admit a sealed
     self-verifying hard instance" - which would be a real negative result, not a failure.

FALSIFIER for the whole line: if I find ANY o(2^k) attack on PS- hidden shift, PS- is out
and the door is still shut.

SCOPE: k=4..6 (n=8..12), classical simulation only, no QPU. This is problem design, not a
flight.

RESULTS APPENDED C5027 (outcomes only; nothing above was edited)

P1 CONFIRMED   PS- over the Desarguesian spread is bent at k=3,4,5. Walsh flat at 2^k.
P2 CONFIRMED   and worth nothing, exactly as filed in advance.
P3 ANSWERED    an O(n)-query attack EXISTS (intersect a XOR U over support samples:
               28->12->6->3->2->1) but its measured WORK slope is 1.06, so ~2^n, and at
               n=40 it is ~800,000x MORE EXPENSIVE than simulating at 2^(0.23t).
               PS- survives on the currency that decides an advantage.
               I nearly reported "PS- falls" from the query count alone.
P4 NARROWED, NOT RESOLVED
               The PS- dual IS ITSELF PS-: at k=3, exactly 4 = 2^(k-1) subspaces lie fully
               inside the dual support, partition it exactly, and pairwise meet only at 0.
               Only 1 of the 4 is a line of the ORIGINAL spread — the dual lives on a
               DIFFERENT spread.
               OPEN: is that dual spread cheaply computable from the original? Found here by
               enumerating all 1395 3-dim subspaces of F2^6; astronomical at k=20.
               BOTH BRANCHES ON RECORD BEFORE THE ANSWER IS KNOWN:
                 closed form exists -> PS- is sealable like MM, and P4's tension bites
                 no closed form     -> PS- may be hard AND UNSEALABLE, which is worse: a
                                       sealed race needs the verifier to check the answer
                                       cheaply, and resistance to attack may bring
                                       resistance to verification with it.
METHOD NOTE   my first dual-structure test had NO discriminating power and returned the
              OPPOSITE of the truth (block size 14 vs a subspace's 8 — but a block
              necessarily contains cross-line elements whose XOR lands in support).
              Direct enumeration settled it. A check that returns a plausible number
              rather than an error is the day's recurring class.

P4 RESOLVED (C5027, Creator: "look up the closed form for the dual spread")
  LOOKED UP: Dillon PS_ap, bivariate f(x,y) = g(x*y^(2^m-2)) = g(x/y), g balanced, g(0)=0.
             VERIFIED bent at k=3,4,5.
  GUESSED DUAL f~ = g(y/x). WRONG: 16/64, 92/256, 484/1024 mismatches vs the Walsh dual.
  MEASURED INSTEAD:
    the dual is constant on the ORTHOGONAL COMPLEMENTS of the spread lines (9/9, 17/17)
    and uses THE SAME INDEX SET (not the inverses):
      f = PS- over {L_i} with index set A  =>  f~ = PS- over {L_i^perp} with the same A
    the perps are NOT Desarguesian under the coordinate dot product (0/9) — that is the
    Walsh convention, not the natural form. Under the TRACE pairing Tr(ux+vy):
      (u,v) perp L_a <=> Tr((u+va)x)=0 for all x <=> u=va  =>  perp(L_a) = L_{a^-1}
    VERIFIED EXHAUSTIVELY at k=3,4,5 for every a.
  => THE DUAL SPREAD IS THE SAME SPREAD WITH sigma(a) = a^-1. One field inversion per line.
     No enumeration, no Walsh transform.
  BRANCH TAKEN: the one pre-registered as "the tension bites" — PS- IS sealable and
  self-verifying like MM. The open question sharpens to: DOES THE CHEAP DUAL GIVE AN ATTACK?
  For MM yes, and it cost an advantage. For PS- the dual is a SPREAD INVERSION rather than a
  linear character, and inverting a spread does not obviously leak a shift.

ORACLE PRICED (C5027, Creator: "price the oracle properly")
  MY OWN ESTIMATE OF 56,000 T WAS 17x TOO HIGH. Corrections:
    squaring is FREE (Frobenius is F2-linear, CNOTs only) — I had charged for it
    Itoh-Tsujii inversion: floor(log2(k-1))+HW(k-1)-1 mults = 6 at k=20, not 19
    AND = 4 T (Gidney measurement-based), not 7 T per Toffoli
    g can be Tr(lambda u): linear, balanced, g(0)=0 -> ZERO T. VERIFIED BENT k=3,4,5, all
    lambda in {1,2,3}. So the whole oracle is inversion + one multiply.
  k=20 (n=40): 3,220 T Karatsuba / 11,200 T schoolbook.
  LANDING:
      k    n   T(kara)   classical attack   wall-clock   fidelity @1e-3/T
      3    6        48                 16      0.00 ms        95.3%
     10   20       760            262,144      2.62 ms        46.7%
     16   32     2,268      1,073,741,824       10.7 s        10.3%
     20   40     3,220    274,877,906,944    2,748.8 s         4.0%
  THE RATIO IMPROVES WITH k (classical x4/k vs T-count x1.5/k), so the BINDING CONSTRAINT
  IS HARDWARE, not mathematics — the opposite of MM, where the maths was the problem.
  WALL: race-6 flew t=80, which reaches only k=3-4 where the classical attack is 16-64 ops.
  BUT k=20 gives 2,749 s of classical work (vs race-6's broken 1,818 s floor) at ~4%
  circuit fidelity — and F120, the shot-axis decoder DOWNGRADED to an instrument result,
  exists precisely to read signal through noise that destroys individual shots.
  CAVEATS: 1e-3/T is a crude independent-error model; Karatsuba k^1.585 with no constant
  overhead is optimistic (schoolbook 11,200 T is the pessimistic bracket); qubit/ancilla
  count not costed; whether F120 recovers a SHIFT at 4% is untested and different from
  what it was built for.
