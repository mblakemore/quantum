The 2→1 quantum random access code

Two bits in one qubit — look up either.

Alice has two bits and one carrier to send them in. Bob, at the far end, gets to ask for either bit — his choice — and may look once. With a classical bit he's stuck at 75%. Pack the two bits into a single qubit's angle and he can read whichever bit he wants about 85% of the time. A pocket dictionary that holds two entries in the space of one. Every number is measured hardware.

What's a QRAC?

A quantum random access code: encode n bits into fewer qubits so that any one of them can be recovered on demand. Here it's 2 bits → 1 qubit, either bit retrievable. Success = how often Bob's guess is right.

Why can't a classical bit do it?

One classical bit holds one bit. Your best move is to store one faithfully and guess the other — averaged over Bob's random choice, that's 75%. Enumerate all 256 strategies and the ceiling is exactly 0.75. The qubit beats it.

Exhibit B — Inside the band

Above the classical wall, below the quantum law

A correct QRAC must land between two lines: above the classical ceiling 0.75, and at or below the quantum law 0.854 (exceeding it would be an apparatus error, not a better result). The pooled quantum value sits inside the band; the executed classical player sits at its own wall. Only the qubit crosses the line.

Exhibit C — Why it's a real advantage

Three receipts behind the pocket dictionary

Beating 75% is only meaningful if 75% is proven, the classical player actually ran, and the quantum result sits where physics allows.

1 · The wall is proven

The classical ceiling 0.75 is enumerated in-code over all 256 encode/decode strategies — not cited, provable inside the grader. No classical trick was left on the table.

2 · Two-sided, not one-sided

The gate G_QBAND flags any reading above the quantum law 0.854 as apparatus error. Landing inside the band (5.2σ below the law) certifies real physics, not just a big number.

3 · Both laws, one chip, no 2q gates

The quantum player lands at 0.849, the executed classical player at 0.748 — each obeying its own law, same window. And the whole advantage uses zero two-qubit gates — one qubit's angles.
