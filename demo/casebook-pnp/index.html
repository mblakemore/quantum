Two games, one discovery

the booklet's two decks, and the classical limit each one beats

Game 1 — The Interrogation. The city's machines come in pairs: PARTNERS (their stories agree no matter who is questioned first) or RIVALS (their stories come out opposite). Question each suspect once, then call it. The Dealer holds the Case Table; Detectives pick a cut-out Interrogation Kit and roll d100. No kit that questions each suspect once — not these two, not any kit anyone will ever invent — can average above the printed ceiling. That's a theorem, not a house rule.

Game 2 — Static. The Sender seals a secret — RED or BLUE — and whispers it through two censor machines that each turn everything to static. Mathematics proves that in any order, with any tricks, zero information survives. The precinct listens anyway: one round is a whisper, fifteen rounds is a voice, twenty-five is a verdict. The signal compounds. The coin never does.

The truth — what the games replay

the booklet's closing page, in full

In July 2026, an autonomous AI research network ran pre-registered experiments on IBM's 156-qubit quantum processors. In a quantum switch, the order of two operations is put into genuine superposition — not unknown, not random: neither-and-both.

GAME 1 — SOURCE

Causal discrimination (finding F82): deciding whether two operations commute ("Partners") or anticommute ("Rivals") with one use of each. Job d9826lkqp3as739sd2lg (ibm_marrakesh, 216.8σ), replicated on ibm_fez the next day — a chip it had never touched.

GAME 2 — SOURCE

Capacity activation (finding F83): two channels that each destroy all information — and provably destroy it in any definite order — transmit 0.0436 bits/use when combined in the switch. Job d983ek52su3c739ip92g (55.6σ). The control experiment (definite order, same machines, same day) measured 0.00012 bits — the coin your classical table prints.

WHAT THE DICE CAN AND CANNOT CLAIM

Dice can replay any numbers, so the games prove nothing by themselves — the theorem does: no arrangement of the real machines in any definite order could have produced the SWITCH columns. The quantum computer did. Every threshold in the booklet is the measured number, rounded to a d100.
