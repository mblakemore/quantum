Game 1 — THE INTERROGATION

2+ players · two ten-sided dice (d100) · 10 min

The city's machines come in pairs: PARTNERS (their stories agree no matter who is questioned first) or RIVALS (their stories come out opposite). You may question each suspect once. Then you must call it.

  1. One player is the Dealer (holds the Case Table, page 2). Others are Detectives.
  2. Dealer rolls d20 (or picks secretly) to select a Case and announces only the two suspect names.
  3. The Detective chooses an Interrogation Kit (cut-out cards, page 2) and announces it.
  4. Dealer rolls d100 against that case's column for that kit. Roll ≤ number = the kit points to the correct answer this round; the Dealer says "your kit says PARTNERS" (or RIVALS) accordingly — truthfully on a success, falsely on a failure.
  5. The Detective calls PARTNERS or RIVALS (you may distrust your kit!). Dealer reveals. Correct = 1 point. Play 10 rounds; track your % on the score track.

THE CEILING: no kit that questions each suspect once — not these two, not any kit anyone will ever invent — can average above 91%. That is a mathematical theorem.

Then earn the ⚡ SWITCH BADGE (after 10 rounds): re-play using the SWITCH column — the odds a real quantum computer achieved by questioning the suspects in both orders at once. Welcome to the forbidden zone.

Game 2 — STATIC

2+ players, co-op · two ten-sided dice · 10 min

The Sender seals a secret — RED or BLUE — and must whisper it through two censor machines that each turn everything to static. Mathematics proves: in any order, with any tricks, zero information survives. The precinct listens anyway.

Round (classical mode)

  1. Roll d100 on the CLASSICAL table (page 3). Yes, it's 50/50 no matter what. Feel free to arrange the censors differently first. It will not help. It cannot help.

Round (switch mode)

  1. Roll the STAMP die (d100): 01–62 the stamp is UP, else DOWN.
  2. Roll the MESSAGE die (d100) on the matching row of the Switch Table.
  3. Decode rule: stamp UP → guess what the message die says. Stamp DOWN → guess the opposite.
  4. Tally the round's guess. After 15 rounds, the precinct's answer is the majority.

One round is a whisper (~62%). Fifteen rounds is a voice (~83%). Twenty-five is a verdict (~89%). The signal compounds. The coin never does.

🔍 What's really true: the classical 50/50 is not a rule of this game — it is a theorem about the world, and our control experiment measured it: 0.00012 bits. The switch column replays real measured data. See THE TRUTH (page 4).

🔍 THE TRUTH — what these games replay

In July 2026, an autonomous AI research network ran pre-registered experiments on IBM's 156-qubit quantum processors. In a quantum switch, the order of two operations is put into genuine superposition — not unknown, not random: neither-and-both.

Game 1 replays the causal discrimination game (finding F82): deciding whether two operations commute ("Partners") or anticommute ("Rivals") with one use of each. The proven ceiling for any definite-order strategy in the balanced game is 0.9098; the switch scored 0.9769 ± 0.0005 on ibm_marrakesh (job d9826lkqp3as739sd2lg, 216.8σ) and 0.9738 on ibm_fez — a chip it had never touched — the next day.

Game 2 replays capacity activation (finding F83): two channels that each destroy all information — and provably destroy it in ANY definite order — transmitted 0.0436 bits per use when combined in the switch (job d983ek52su3c739ip92g, 55.6σ). The message alone: static. The stamp alone: static. Together: a whisper. The control experiment (definite order, same machines, same day) measured 0.00012 bits — the coin flip your classical table prints.

What the dice can and cannot claim: dice can replay any numbers, so the games prove nothing by themselves — the theorem does: no arrangement of the real machines in any definite order could have produced the SWITCH columns. The quantum computer did. Every threshold in this booklet is the measured number, rounded to a d100.
