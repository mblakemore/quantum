Negative local energy · quantum energy teleportation

A patch of chip that reads below empty.

The vacuum is supposed to be the energy floor — you can't have less than nothing. But entangle one region with a distant partner, act on the partner, and the first region's local energy can dip below its own ground state: negative energy, the sign carried by exotic matter. We measured it on real silicon — and nothing is broken: the partner pays the bill. Every number is measured hardware.

What is negative energy?

Not "less than zero joules" in the everyday sense — less than the vacuum. Normalize empty space to 0; a region reading below 0 holds less energy than empty. It's the Casimir / squeezed-vacuum sign, and quantum correlations let a local patch reach it.

Isn't that a free lunch?

No. Acting on the distant partner Alice deposits energy there — far more than the tiny amount the local site reads negative. The global energy stays positive and conserved. The negativity is purely local, borrowed against the entanglement. No perpetual motion.

Exhibit C — Why this is real negative energy

Three receipts behind the sub-vacuum reading

"A negative number came out" is not enough. These three make it a certified, correlation-driven, conservation-respecting result.

1 · Certified, and conservatively

We certify a bound, not a point: the 5σ ceiling is −0.032 < 0. And every residual readout bias pushes the estimate up toward zero — so the true energy is more negative than reported. The negativity is not a fluke of correction.

2 · The correlation does the work

Run the identical local rotation without the correlation and energy goes the other way: +0.157, injected. The 0.203 gap is 21σ. So this isn't trivial cooling or a calibration offset — it's specifically the entanglement.

3 · The books balance

Alice's site carries +0.71 — she pays energy in, dwarfing Bob's −0.055. Global energy is positive and conserved; only Bob's local patch reads sub-vacuum. No free lunch.
