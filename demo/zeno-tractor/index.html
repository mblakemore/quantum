The quantum Zeno effect

Hold a qubit still by watching it.

Aim a full π-rotation at a qubit and it should flip from ON to OFF. But keep measuring it as it turns, and it freezes in place — the act of looking is a tractor beam. Turn up how often you look and watch the survival climb… until the frontier, where each glance costs enough that looking faster stops paying. Every number is measured hardware data — on the cheapest circuit of the whole campaign.

Quantum Zeno is textbook (Misra–Sudarshan 1977). It pins against coherent rotation, not relaxation. Contribution: the QND-corrected law + the measured frontier.

Exhibit B — The Cadence Law & the Frontier

Watch faster, hold tighter — but each look costs

The dashed curve is the ideal Zeno law, [cos²(π/2N)]^N: watch infinitely fast and the qubit never flips. The dots are measured. Divide out the per-look cost and they match the law to 0.5% through N=8 — but the raw survival plateaus at N=16, the watch-cost frontier, where each glance's cost cancels the extra grip.
