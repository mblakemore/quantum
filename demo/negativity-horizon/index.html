Where the failures sit

The 30 certified sets run from mean visibility 0.422 to 0.677. The 10 exceptions run from 0.308 to 0.398. The two ranges do not overlap.

The split is not at the horizon. For an intact channel at uniform visibility c the least Bell-diagonal eigenvalue is λmin = (1 − 3c) / 4, negative only above c = 1/3 — and 1/3 is 0.333, which sits inside the exception band, not between the two groups. c = 1/3 is where negativity ceases to exist.

The boundary you can actually see is where negativity ceases to be certifiable by us. Derived rather than asserted: the weakest certified set has |λmin| = 0.0665 at 5.4σ, so se(λ) ≈ 0.0123; a 5σ bar therefore needs |λ| ≥ 0.0616, which (1−3c)/4 places at c ≈ 0.415 — inside the observed gap of 0.398 to 0.422. That boundary is our noise floor, not a law.

Which means the band between 1/3 and 0.415 holds sets with genuine temporal negativity that this analysis cannot certify — and the page's own numbers show them: 8 of the 10 exceptions have negative λmin, real signal below the bar rather than absence of signal. An earlier version of this page said the split simply was c = 1/3. It was not, and the difference is a detection limit promoted into a physical law.

Checked rather than quoted: over all 80 re-fly rows the median |λmin − (1−3c̄)/4| is 0.00000. The identity holds to the stored precision, which is what makes “the exceptions are the sets near 1/3” a derivation rather than an observation.

Two layers, and why the compass did not care

The certified sets sit at λmin -0.258 to -0.067, at 5.4σ to 25.6σ below zero. The exceptions sit at +0.0190 to -0.0485 — too small to certify at the 5σ bar.

Sign structure is what the compass reads, and it survives depolarization. Temporal negativity is that same structure's strong-visibility limit, and it dies at the horizon. The exceptions carry the intact sign pattern: the compass still called them correctly. The witness could not certify them. Those are different questions, and only one of them has a horizon.
