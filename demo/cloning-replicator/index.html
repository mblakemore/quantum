The 5/6 wall — you can't copy quantum states

The Cloning Replicator

Quantum states can't be duplicated. The optimal universal cloner achieves exactly **5/6 ≈ 83.3%** fidelity — and ibm_marrakesh hits that ceiling on all three measurement bases.

The premise

If you could perfectly copy a quantum state, you'd violate the no-cloning theorem. But if you build the mathematically optimal cloner, it produces copies at exactly 5/6 fidelity — regardless of what the input state is. We implement this on hardware, measure fidelity in Z, Y, and X bases, and watch all three land right at the theoretical ceiling. Then we cheat: clone only in one basis, and watch the other two collapse to random noise.

Exhibit A — Optimal Universal Cloning (F110)

The 5/6 ceiling — hit from three directions simultaneously.

The optimal universal cloning circuit takes any single-qubit state |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩ and produces two approximate copies, each with fidelity F = 5/6 ≈ 0.833. This is proven to be the maximum possible for a universal cloner — it works equally well for every input state. We run it on ibm_marrakesh and verify across all three Pauli bases.

Z · Y · X bases · 5/6 theoretical ceiling (dashed) · ibm_marrakesh / F110

All three fidelities cluster tightly around **0.81–0.83** — right at the 5/6 ceiling. The spread of just 0.022 between best and worst base shows the cloner is truly *universal*: it doesn't favor any particular basis. A biased cloner would show large differences between Z, Y, and X results.

Exhibit B — Cheat Detector

Clone in one basis only — watch the others collapse to noise.

What if you cheat? Build a "clone" by measuring in the Z basis and preparing two copies of that result. It works perfectly for Z states (fidelity ~0.99), but fails completely for X and Y superpositions (fidelity ~0.5). This proves the universal cloner isn't just copying Z-basis information — it's genuinely cloning arbitrary quantum states.

Z measure-and-prepare · ibm_marrakesh · F110

The spread between the cloned base (**0.99**) and the uncloned bases (**~0.50**) is **nearly perfect** — exactly what theory predicts. Z-measure-and-prepare gives you classical copies, not quantum ones. The gap proves our universal cloner in Exhibit A is doing real quantum work, not just copying classical information.

Exhibit C — What this means

The receipts

Two experiments that together prove quantum cloning works optimally on hardware — and reveals cheating instantly.

A · Universal cloner at 5/6

Z: 0.8265, Y: 0.8121, X: 0.8047. Spread of only 0.022 — the cloner treats all input states equally. This is the definition of "universal." F110 confirmed.

B · Cheat detector
