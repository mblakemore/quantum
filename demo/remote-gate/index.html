The Remote Handshake.

Teleportation usually moves a noun — a state from here to there. This moves a verb. Two data qubits that never touch have an entangling CNOT performed between them, using one shared Bell pair and two classical messages. It's the primitive behind distributed and fault-tolerant quantum computers — running a gate across a link you can't put both qubits on. And it hides a beautiful catch: the test for "did it work" turns out to be the exact line between a real quantum gate and a fake one.

Scope: a nonlocal CNOT (Eisert–Jozsa–Simon: 1 e-bit + 2 classical bits, feedforward both ways) on one die — the gate-teleportation primitive, not a compiled distributed algorithm or a logical gate.

THE WITNESS IS THE QUANTUM–CLASSICAL LINE

Strip the shared entanglement (the "no resource" arm) and the gate still passes the truth table (0.885) — because a CNOT's classical action is just a permutation of basis states, and you can do that with classical messages alone. But its Bell fidelity caps at 1/2 (0.454): ZZ survives, XX and YY collapse to zero. So F > 1/2 isn't a "did it work" threshold — it is precisely the boundary a fake gate cannot cross. Only the real one does. the e-bit is the quantum resource, shown in data

THE PROTOCOL

One Bell pair shared between the two sides, and two classical bits. Alice CNOTs her data into her half and measures it → sends one bit. Bob corrects, CNOTs into his data, measures in the X basis → sends one bit back. Alice corrects. Net: CNOT(A→B), with A and B never coupled. Minimal: 1 e-bit, 2 c-bits.

THE PRICE

The teleported gate reaches Bell F = 0.789 (25σ past the witness); the same-job direct CNOT reads 0.979. The 0.19 gap is the two feedforward-idle windows — the qubits wait, and dephase, while the classical bits fly. The gate is real; the tax is latency, the same idle class the rest of this wing pays.
