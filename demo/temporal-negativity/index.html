A density matrix that goes below zero.

The numbers along a density matrix's diagonal are probabilities, and its eigenvalues are too. They cannot be negative — that is not a rule of thumb, it is what it means for a description to describe something. Point the same estimator at one qubit measured at two moments and it assembles a matrix whose smallest eigenvalue is −0.478. Half a probability below zero. Nothing in space can produce those statistics; only something that visited two times can.

Why the reading is impossible for space

Three correlators, measured in both arms by the same pipeline

A two-qubit state has to make a trade. Push the X-correlation to +1 and the Y-correlation is forced negative — the maximally entangled states cap at a (+, −, +) sign pattern up to a local change of frame. All three positive at once is not a strong state; it is no state.

The temporal arm reads +0.972, +0.979, +0.962 — three positives, the pattern no bipartite state carries. The same estimator pointed at an actual Φ⁺ pair reads +0.956, −0.943, +0.938 and assembles into a matrix that stays at or above zero, exactly as a state must.

The instrument was given a chance to fail on something known, and did not take it.
