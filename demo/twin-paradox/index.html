The quantum twin paradox · Zych–Brukner analog

The twin who ages loses the interference.

Einstein's twins: the one who travels ages less. Make it quantum — send a particle down two paths at once so it interferes with itself, and hang a clock on it. If the clock ages, its reading quietly records which path was taken, and that record destroys the interference. We built the analog on a chip: an excited clock (it ages) washes out coherence far faster than a vacuum clock. Every number is measured hardware.

What's an interferometer?

A device that splits one particle onto two paths at once and recombines it, so it interferes with itself. The interference only appears if the two paths are indistinguishable — the instant anything records which way it went, the pattern vanishes.

Why does "aging" matter?

A clock that ticks while in flight ends up reading how much time passed on its path. If the two paths age differently, the clock is a which-path record — so aging itself decoheres the superposition. That's the twin paradox as quantum information.

Exhibit C — How we know it's aging, not a trick

Three receipts behind the verdict

A big number is only worth as much as the audit behind it. This one was demoted by its own author, isolated, and re-earned. Here's how the confounds were closed.

1 · Rotation can't fake it

We read |V| = √(X²+Y²), the length of the coherence vector. A stray phase rotation only spins that vector — it can't shrink it. Only genuine decoherence can. Phase-blind by construction.

2 · The obvious mechanism, refuted

The pre-filed guess was a static ZZ pull, which a spin-echo should undo. The echo recovery came back −0.119 — the wrong sign. Static-ZZ REFUTED, in the open, against the author's own 0.80 prior.

3 · The withheld 67σ

The first run passed at 67σ — bigger than the 36σ reported — but its X-curves went negative (impossible for a real visibility). The author withheld it and ran this phase-blind retest instead. The asterisk was self-attached.
