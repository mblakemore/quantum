A causal game, and the ruler chosen in the open

Two unknown operations. Do they commute, or anticommute? You may use each one once. Any machine that runs them in a definite order — first this, then that, even choosing the order as it goes — has a hard ceiling on how often it can answer correctly. A process with indefinite order does not. This exhibit is that game, played blind against a sealed answer sheet, and it is mostly about the part nobody exhibits: how the winning margin was measured, and which of four defensible rulers the court decided to use.

Scope: this is not a new physics result, and the card that reports it says so first. The discrimination win belongs to an earlier result. What this rung adds is a blind sealed court and an explicit task framing — a way of grading a win, not a new one.

The game

One use of each operation, and a promise that they either commute or do not

Two operations A and B are drawn from a fixed set of ten. You are promised they either commute (AB = BA) or anticommute (AB = −BA). You must say which — and you may apply each of them exactly once. The catch is that with one use each, the order you apply them in is the only lever you have, and a definite order tells you less than you need.

Every machine that applies them in some definite order is bounded. That includes a machine that fixes the order in advance, one that flips a coin, and one that decides the order after seeing an intermediate outcome. All of those live inside a single mathematical object — the causally-separable set — and the best any of them can do on this game, under the flown input distribution, is 0.869028. That number was re-derived in code at freeze time, not quoted from the earlier result it certifies, with a primal–dual bracket of 2.12e-08 certifying it independently of the solver's own status flag.

The quantum switch does not fix an order. Routed through it, both orders are present in a way that no definite-order machine reproduces, and the flown result was 0.988383 — above the ceiling by 0.119355. That gap, divided by an uncertainty, is the whole claim; which uncertainty, and which gap, is the next panel.

What the chip actually is The processor runs in a fixed causal order. The switch is realised by controlled routing, and the query currency is controlled-calls at the process-abstraction level. Enforced single-firing is not available in the gate model. This is a certified implementation of the game, not a demonstration that the laboratory's causal order was indefinite — and device-independent versions of this scenario class are provably impossible, so no amount of care would upgrade it to one.

Four rulers, one published

A significance is a comparison, and a comparison has two ends

A number like "74.0 sigma" reads as a property of the result. It is not. It is a ratio: how far the measurement sits above a ceiling, divided by how uncertain that distance is. Change either end honestly and the number changes — and for this flight there were four defensible ways to set them, giving answers from 3.16 to 237.2. The grading seat ruled on which one counted before choosing between them, wrote the rejected ones into the record with their would-be numbers, and published the smaller of the two it accepted. The oath — the order is the protocol

A step performed out of sequence voids the seal, whatever the numbers say

The result is blind: the answer sheet was sealed by a seat that did not fly the experiment, and the flight never received it. What makes that more than an assurance is that each step is stamped by an artefact that could not have been produced later.

  1. A sealing seat draws and seals the instance sequence — which pairs, in which order.
  2. The commitment digest is published to the bus before flight, fixing the answer sheet.
  3. The flight runs — 51 ordered pairs, 1,000 shots each — and never receives the sequence.
  4. The records are decoded blind, against a grader frozen in advance.
  5. The decisions hash is published before the unseal: a0984134f583ec633309d82999309aae2dbc7a6f5763d6bbc5e94341f04866c9
  6. Only then is the sequence unsealed and revealed against its commitment.

The outcome of that decode: 51/51 pair decisions correct, 0 mismatches, seal intact through the full order. The measured separation was 1.9080 against an ideal of 2.0000.

A gate that cannot fail is not a gate Every check in this rung carries a positive control — including the attack review itself, at which a deliberately all-yes claim was fired and correctly blocked. That is why "all classes clear" is reported here as a decision rather than as silence. One attack class was discovered by this rung: a hash binds bytes, not their parse, and two legal readings of the sealed artefact produced canonical orders differing in every one of 51 positions with every hash still green. It was fixed with a pinned lexicographic rule and an index-table digest, and then swept across the whole campaign.

What this is not

Taken from the card's own opening section, which puts them before the number

1 · Not a new column on the scoreboard The discrimination win is the earlier result's. This rung adds exactly two things: a blind sealed court, and an explicit computational-task framing. The card states in its first section that a reader who takes it as a fresh physics result has been misled by it, and that this would be a defect in the card. The exhibit inherits that sentence rather than softening it.
2 · The sharpest open question, unresolved The ceiling here bounds definite-order machines without controlled access. The symmetric-access ceiling — the same bound when the definite-order side is granted controlled access too — does not exist, and this result does not imply it is small. What is banked is only a narrowing: controlled access cannot manufacture the opposite ordering. That is a constraint on access, not a ceiling.
3 · Which is why there is no scoreboard row The campaign's scoreboard framing for this result was drafted at freeze and is explicitly marked NOT POSTABLE until that gate is resolved. It has not been resolved, so this exhibit does not claim a scoreboard position and the museum's scoreboard does not carry one. A page that quietly promoted it would be publishing past a fence its own authors set.
