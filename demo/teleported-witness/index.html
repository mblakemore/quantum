Beaming indefinite order across the chip.

A quantum switch holds two operations in both orders at once — a real physical resource, carried by a single control qubit. Here we teleport that qubit one hop before reading its witness, and ask: does the indefiniteness arrive intact? Over a genuine entangled channel it does — 97% of the un-teleported value. Over a classical channel, the exact same protocol, it dies. Every number is measured hardware.

What's a causal witness?

One measurable number, DISC. It reads 0 for any ordinary sequence of events — even a random coin-flip between "A first" and "B first" — and climbs toward 2 the more the order is genuinely indefinite. Nonzero DISC is something no definite order can fake.

What's teleportation here?

Standard quantum teleportation — a shared entangled pair plus two classical bits move a qubit's state from A to B with no wire carrying the qubit. No faster-than-light anything. The novelty is what we send: the carrier of indefinite causal order itself.

Exhibit C — Why this is transmission, not survival

Three receipts behind the double win

"The witness survived teleportation" would be a weak claim on its own. These three make it a strong one.

1 · The classical null was run

The dead 0.0175 isn't a theory line — it's the identical teleport over a dephased resource, measured in the same job and window. That executed null is what turns "survived noise" into "carried by entanglement."

2 · Four predictions, four hits

All four pre-filed predictions landed — including the subtle one: active feedforward costs a little (1.766 < 1.825), appearing in a 4th observable family. The noise model had guessed the opposite; the chip corrected it.

3 · The grader proved itself first

This was the first flight under the R5 rule: the grading code had to pass a synthetic self-test before it was allowed to touch the hardware counts. No moving the goalposts after the data landed.
