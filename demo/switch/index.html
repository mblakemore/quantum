30-second primer

What the control qubit is doing

Two operations on a target, and a control qubit that steers which order they run in. Put the control in a superposition and the two orders happen coherently at once.

the setup

A control qubit |c⟩ routes a target through two gates. |0⟩ → order B·A, |1⟩ → order A·B. Prepare |c⟩ = (|0⟩+|1⟩)/√2 and both orders run superposed.

the readout

Measure the control in the X basis. Its value ⟨X_c⟩ depends on whether A and B commute (order-agnostic) or anticommute (order-flipping). The control encodes which order mattered.

the witness

DISC = ⟨X_c⟩commute − ⟨X_c⟩anticommute. A coherent switch gives ≈ +2. Any classical mixture of definite orders gives ≈ 0. That gap is the proof.

Interactive · the coherence dial

Drain the order-coherence and watch the witness fall

φ dephases the control's order-basis coherence: φ = 0 is the fully coherent switch, φ = π is a classical 50/50 coin flip between the two orders. The theory says the witness should trace DISC(φ) = 2·cos(φ/2). On ibm_kingston it did — to Pearson 0.9992. The five amber dots are real measurements; the line is that confirmed law.

The rigor · closing the loophole

Beating the hardest classical adversary, in one calibration window

A skeptic's best objection: "a classical process that randomly picks order B·A or A·B also has access to the commutator." So the real test isn't switch-vs-spectator — it's switch vs a genuine classical mixture of definite orders. What it isn't

What this is — and what it isn't

Effective process, not physical indefinite order

ibm_marrakesh is a fixed-causal-order chip. The switch is realized by controlled routing on a definite-order circuit — an operational witness of order-coherence, not a claim that the hardware itself has an indefinite past.

Order-coherence, not a speed-up

This witnesses that the control encodes which order was applied (an interference signature). It is not a demonstrated computational or query-complexity advantage of the quantum switch.

The shape is the claim, not the amplitude

Hardware amplitude-damping reads DISC a few percent low of the ideal (φ=0 measured +1.936 vs +2.000). The load-bearing results are the sign, the cosine shape (Pearson 0.9992), and the ≥72σ separation — all robust to that attenuation.

How the circuit actually works (for the curious)

The control qubit conditions the order of two target unitaries A and B. With |c⟩ coherent, the reduced target state is A·B|ψ⟩ on one branch and B·A|ψ⟩ on the other, superposed. Reading the control in X interferes the branches: the interference term carries the commutator [A,B], so ⟨X_c⟩ reports commute (+) vs anticommute (−).

The dial's φ applies a partial controlled-rotation cry(φ) onto an untraced ancilla — a tunable which-order copy. Tracing the ancilla multiplies the control's order-basis coherence by cos(φ/2), so the witness scales as DISC(φ) = 2·cos(φ/2). φ = 0 leaves the switch intact; φ = π is a complete which-order copy ≡ the classical 50/50 mixture. F74 derived this law in simulation; F76 confirmed it on ibm_kingston at Pearson 0.9992.
