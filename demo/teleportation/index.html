A discovery from the Heron 2026 campaign

Teleportation.

Destroy a qubit's state here and rebuild it there — carried by a shared entangled pair and two classical bits, with the original provably gone (no-cloning). Not a copy: the state crosses without anything that holds it ever traversing the gap. We beamed six states across real silicon and checked every one against what we sent.

Scope: single-hop, real feed-forward, finite fidelity (SPAM + 1 entangling gate + correction latency). A hardware demonstration of the primitive, not a deployed network.

1 · Six states, beamed and verified

ibm_fez · the six cardinal states · fidelity of the received state vs the one we sent

A classical strategy — measure the qubit, send the result, rebuild — cannot exceed an average fidelity of 2/3 (a theorem; measurement destroys what a superposition holds). Beat 2/3 and only genuine quantum teleportation explains it. Every bar clears the line; the average is 0.913.

2 · Two falsifiers, two resources

Teleportation needs two things: the shared entanglement and the classical correction. Remove either and it must fail — and each fails differently, so no partial cheat can pass both. Switch the runs above: no entanglement kills the superposition states (X/Y → chance; the Z states survive by accident, a qubit left near |0⟩ answers |0⟩ right). No correction kills everything (every state → chance), because the received qubit is right only up to a random Pauli the classical bits were supposed to undo.

Genuine teleportation is the only thing that clears 2/3 and survives both falsifiers. This run does: 0.913 average, entanglement-off collapses the superpositions, correction-off collapses all six.
