Telling a cause from a common cause, without intervening

Two things move together. Did the first cause the second, or did something else cause both? Given only observations — no ability to reach in and change anything — those two stories predict the same data. That is not a gap in the statistics; for two variables it is a theorem. This exhibit is a blind test of an instrument that was asked to tell them apart anyway, and a place to grade its answers yourself.

75/75 blind calls correct CC 37/37 CE 38/38 clears its pre-registered 5 sigma bar n=1 window a re-fly

Scope: an instrument result, not a physics claim. It establishes that a frozen statistic separated two labelled populations — not that the labels mean what the arc calls them.

The thing that is supposed to be impossible

Rung one of the ladder, doing rung two's job

In Pearl's ladder of causation, rung one is seeing and rung two is doing. The classic result is that seeing is not enough: with two correlated variables and purely observational data, "A causes B" and "a hidden C causes both" are observationally equivalent — no amount of watching separates them. You have to intervene.

Quantum mechanics offers a seam. A genuine cause-effect chain — measure, wait, measure the same system again — is forced by measurement repeatability toward all-positive correlations on the diagonal, because the first measurement projects the system onto an eigenstate. A common cause — two systems prepared together and measured separately — is under no such constraint. Classically neither arrangement is forced, which is exactly why the classical problem is stuck. The discriminating work here is done by that causal-structure argument, not by the size of any correlation.

The sentence this exhibit is not allowed to say "The sign flip is the quantum signature" — that is FALSE, and the frozen protocol requires it be written down as false: a classical shared-variable model reproduces the same sign pattern exactly. The true statement is narrower: within quantum mechanics a cause-effect chain is forced to all-positive diagonals by measurement repeatability, while a common cause is not — and classically neither is forced. The discrimination rests on that asymmetry, not on a dramatic-looking sign.
Prior art, stated plainly The concept is not ours: quantum causal discovery of this kind is Ried et al., Nature Physics 11, 414 (2015). What this cell contributes is a protocol and a certification on hardware — a blind, sealed, pre-registered run of the idea, not the idea.

    How significant, and significant against what

    The denominator is the whole question, and this page had it wrong first

    A hit rate means nothing until you say what it is being compared to. The billed unit here is blind call-success over sealed, matched records — fixed count, identical in both arms — as the frozen protocol specifies. The comparison is not a coin. It is the enumerated classical ceiling: the best any classical observational model can do on these records, which the protocol fixes as c = 1/2 + d/(2W) for a randomisation band of width W.

    Corrected in the record, in the open The finding first reported 8.66 sigma — and that figure is against a fair coin, which was never the frozen null. It was published, audited, and the wrong denominator survived both reads. The grading seat named the defect as his within minutes of it being raised, derived the enumerated ceiling from the pre-run's raw records, and banked it as its own artefact. The corrected figure is below; the coin number stays on the page because striking it out silently would be its own kind of edit.

    Why "at most", and not a number on its own The frozen protocol takes the maximum of three candidate numerators, and only the first was flown for the re-fly — a permutation-calibrated bound and an executed classical arm were not. A maximum computed over one of three candidates can only be too small, so the ceiling is a lower bound and the significance an upper bound. The correct sentence is therefore "at most 7.9 sigma over the enumerated classical ceiling", and this page will not print it without the qualifier.

    What does not depend on any of those digits. The bar was pre-registered at 5 sigma. Solve for the ceiling at which 75-out-of-75 would fall to exactly 5 sigma: c = n/(n + 25) = 0.75. So the verdict survives any classical ceiling below 0.75 — and the enumerated one is 0.544, nowhere near it. The digit moved; the verdict did not. That invariant is worth more than a larger number would be, because it is the part that cannot be taken away by a better-derived floor.

    Two different sigmas, said before anyone asks The ceiling is built from an inter-arm gap that is itself only a 2.09-sigma effect, and that gap is the input to d. The headline is a different quantity — 75 correct blind calls out of 75, scored against the ceiling that gap produces. Both are true at once, and neither is evidence for the other. A reader who takes "at most 7.9 sigma" to mean the underlying separation is comparably strong has read it wrong, so the page says it here rather than waiting to be asked.

    What the ceiling's second derivation does and does not cover. A second seat re-derived it from the raw records with independently written code and reproduced the banked value and the shapes — that checks the execution. The forms are a separate question, and they are pre-registered: c = 1/2 + d/(2W) with d the maximum of three numerators at their upper bounds, and the standard-error expression, are each cited by line to the frozen pre-registration, so they were fixed before any data existed and could not have been chosen to fit it.

    And the forms have since been checked on their merits, from first principles by a seat outside the work: the correlator standard error, the factor of √2 for a difference of two independent arms, and d = |gap| + 2 SE as a two-sigma upper bound are each correct as written.

    That check found one mismatch, and this page would rather state it than be caught by it. The gap is estimated as a mean over 60 (unit × basis) cells, while the frozen standard error is that of a pooled per-arm correlator. Exact per-cell propagation gives an SE about 43% smaller. The two assume different pooling — but the frozen form is the larger one, so it overstates the ceiling and understates the result: carried through, the figure would be 8.1 sigma rather than 7.9. The pre-registered choice errs against the claim it supports.

    So the published number sits between two conservatisms pointing opposite ways: only one of three numerators was flown, which makes it an upper bound; the pooling choice makes it a lower one. Neither was chosen after seeing the answer. Anyone checking the standard error against the estimator will find that 43% — it is written here so they find it stated rather than discovered.

    Reference only, and not the frozen null: against a fair coin, 75/75 is 8.66 sigma. Kept visible because it is what the finding first carried.

    What this is, and four things it is not

    Every one of these is in the finding's own record, not softened for the wall

    1 · An instrument result, not physics 75 correct calls out of 75 establishes that the statistic separated two labelled populations. It does not establish that the labels mean what the arc claims they mean, and nothing in this apparatus can. The decoder classifies the prepared arms; read it that way.
    2 · This is a re-fly. The first flight was killed by its own author The first attempt was called NO-TEST before decode by the seat that designed it: the randomisation had been implemented as an idle delay, which is dephasing — it kills two axes and spares the third — where the frozen design called for depolarizing, which attenuates all three equally. It would have made the arms separable for a reason that has nothing to do with causal structure. The fix was a weighted Pauli twirl; this page reports the re-fly.
    3 · 75 of 80, and the 5 were withdrawn by the seat who caused it 5 sets (3 CC / 2 CE) were excluded because the sealing seat had run the frozen decoder against real blinded files while checking the format — contaminating them. That was disclosed unprompted, when silence would have been cheap and undetectable, and it was ruled on before the answer was visible rather than discovered afterwards as a relief. Cost: 0.28 sigma of headroom. It changed no verdict.

    The 5 are named in the public record, before the decode. They were posted by id at 18:30:14 — in the same minute as the answer key's digest and 65 seconds before the blind calls were committed as a hash. The ids absent from the 75 decisions are exactly that named set, matched one for one rather than counted: a count check would pass on any five, this pins the identity. Independently confirmed by a third recomputation that reproduced both the seal and the 75/75 from the raw artefacts, and which stated its own limits — it was neither blind nor from a seat outside the court.

    And the rule is checkable on its face as well. The 5 withdrawn ids are exactly the 5 lexicographically smallest of the 80 — the batch a format check picks up first. Their units are scattered (10, 16, 33, 34, 39) and their arms are mixed (3 CC / 2 CE), so the rule is independent of both the data and the answer. Choosing that particular set at random is a 1-in-24-million shot; choosing it to flatter a result is not something anyone would do, because it is the one selection an outsider can reconstruct without asking.
    4 · One window, not three — and the blemish This is n=1: a single blinded science window, d9tg7gntfhrs73dtug20 (2026-08-11). Two earlier jobs belong to the first flight — the one called NO-TEST — and are not windows of this claim. Separately, in the record rather than a footnote: the unseal landed between the decisions hash and a pre-unseal question, so that question received a post-unseal answer. The core ordering held — the calls were already committed as a hash — but the weaker courtesy, that every question be answered blind, did not.
