Delayed-choice quantum eraser · on silicon

A future coin edits the past's fringe.

Mark which path a qubit took and its interference fringe dies. Then — after the qubit is already measured — choose to erase the mark, and the fringe comes back. A choice made in the qubit's future decides whether its past shows a fringe. And yet you can't send a message with it: the raw signal stays flat.

Mark, then un-mark

Copying which-path info onto a marker qubit destroys the system's interference. Erasing that mark (measure the marker in the opposite basis) brings the fringe back — the information, not the particle, is what mattered.

Why it's not time travel

The fringe only appears when you sort the already-recorded data by the marker's later result — you need both records. The raw, unsorted signal is flat (0.055): no message travels back, no faster-than-light.

No retrocausal signalling — the unconditioned marginal is flat (0.055); the delayed choice pays a mapped idle-dephasing tax; one die.

Exhibit B — The no-signalling marginal

It edits the pattern; it cannot send a message

Two numbers carry the result. The delayed-choice signal — how much the future coin toggles the fringe — is +0.642 (coin=1 fringe 0.797 vs coin=0 flat 0.155). The no-signalling marginal — what you'd see without the coincidence sort — is flat at 0.055 everywhere. The past's pattern bends to a future choice; no information does. The flat marginal is the fence and a measured number.

Exhibit B2 — Selecting the past · a second, later flight

Not just whether there was a fringe — which one

Everything above is Exp155. This panel is a different flight, Exp230, and it answers a different question. Exp155 asked whether erasing the mark brings the fringe back (it does). Exp230 asks what the eraser's own outcome decides — and the answer is that the two outcomes give opposite pasts. Note the direction of the comparison plainly: Exp155's DYNAMIC mode is the stronger delayed choice of the two, because its choice is a real quantum coin measured after the fact with feed-forward. Exp230's choice is the standard eraser postselection. It is not "more delayed" — it is a different axis.

The dots are the eight measured phase points, not a fitted curve. V(m=0) = +0.959, V(m=1) = −0.951 — a separation of 1.910, i.e. the two subensembles are near-perfect mirror images. Keep the which-path record instead and it is flat (0.026). And the screen nobody sorted stays flat throughout (0.027): a past can be selected, and still cannot be signalled.

Exhibit C — Why this counts

Three receipts, and the fence

1 · The matched control

The which-path arm pays the identical post-selection cost and stays flat (0.068). So the fringe is the erasure, not the sorting. Not a selection artifact.

2 · The choice is in the future

In DYNAMIC mode a quantum coin is measured after the system is recorded, and a real feed-forward erases the marker only if the coin says so. Causally later.

3 · The fence

No retrocausal signalling: the marginal is flat (0.055). The delayed choice costs a mapped idle-dephasing tax (0.797 vs 0.946). One die.
