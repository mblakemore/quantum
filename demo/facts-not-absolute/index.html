Wigner's friend · observed facts, tested

A fact isn't a fact until the world copies it.

Two "friends" each look at their qubit and write down what they saw — a definite outcome. But while those notes stay private, there's no single answer everyone can agree on: a facts-CHSH test breaks the bound every "the observation is an absolute fact" model must obey. Copy the notes out into the world and the fact locks in.

The friend

The "friend" is a memory qubit that measures its system and records the result (E(F,F) ≈ 0.94 — it genuinely records). This is a theorem about records, not consciousness.

Isolated vs copied

Keep the records isolated and a superobserver can still coherently overrule them → no absolute fact. Copy them into the environment (many copies) and that becomes impossible → the fact is now absolute.
