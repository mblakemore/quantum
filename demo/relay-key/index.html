WHY THE RELAY CANNOT SPY

After its Bell measurement the relay holds no qubit correlated with the key — the swap consumes its particles entirely. Its outcomes are published to everyone, spy included. And S > 2 bounds every eavesdropping strategy at once — not "we didn't detect a spy" but "a spy is impossible above this score." Security by theorem, under E91's standard assumptions — the fence below names what this layer doesn't include.

FRAME-STEERED SIFTING

The relay's corrections are Pauli frames — but key measurements happen at ±45°, where frames can't be XORed away. The conjugation rule saves every shot: a pending frame (x,z) flips the outcome's sign by x and steers Bob's effective angle between ±45° by x⊕z. Each shot lands in a valid CHSH term. THE MODEL THAT FELL

The pre-registered prediction said the two-relay key fails: S = 1.97, from the standard mapping that grades a link by one fidelity number. Measured: 2.235, certified. The decomposition: our links die by lopsided dephasing (ZZ ≫ XX), and for such states S = √2·(ZZ+XX) — the previous night's own correlations already predicted 2.05, a violation hidden by the one-number collapse. The rest (2.05→2.24) was a favorable condition swing. The lopsidedness also keeps key-bit errors low: dephasing-limited links are better key carriers than their fidelity suggests.
