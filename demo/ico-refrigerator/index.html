INDEFINITE-CAUSAL-ORDER THERMODYNAMICSEVERY NUMBER MEASURED

Cooling from the order of operations.

Send a qubit through two thermalizing channels — but put the order of the two channels into a quantum superposition. Read a control qubit, and the target comes out colder or hotter than the baths it touched. No definite-order process can do this: its causal value is exactly zero. Turn the knob and watch it happen — every number is measured hardware data.

Resource identified by Felce–Vedral (2020). The contribution: the frozen-court measurement on silicon.

Exhibit C — Spending the Cold Branch (F118)

Put the cold to work: reset another qubit below the bath

Exhibit A measured the cold branch in place. Here it is spent: after the switch makes the cold outcome, a SWAP delivers it onto a separate data qubit that was never part of the fridge — leaving that qubit at p₁ = 0.2100, below the bath and colder than any definite-order reset on the same warm baths. The engine (Exhibit B) spent the hot branch; this spends the cold one, for the first time in the campaign.

How cold can it go? — the boundary. The ICO resource itself saturates: cascading the cooled qubit back through the switch converges to p₁ ≈ 0.177 — its causal-order floor for these baths, not zero. You can reach ~0.13 by combining several cold qubits with a classical majority vote (measured on-chip, 37.6σ), but that is classical entropy compression that works on any biased qubit — not the ICO resource, and it earns no finding. The quantum part is the sub-bath split; the rest is classical bookkeeping.
