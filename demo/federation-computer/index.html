SOFTWARE BEATS THE LIVE WIRE

Flight 2 flew both welds side by side: a software frame applied after the fact, and live feed-forward via a dynamic circuit. Theory says both are coherent. On this hardware, this generation of dynamic circuits, they were not: SW ⟨X̄X̄⟩ 0.890 vs FF ⟨X̄X̄⟩ 0.339 — the live conditional gate's added latency lets the data qubits decohere while they wait. Post-hoc bookkeeping beat a live classical channel.

ONE ARCHITECTURAL CHANGE UNLOCKED THE WITNESS

Flight 1 put each ebit inside its data block; the code's shared-q0 structure then forbids reading a data qubit and its own ebit in incompatible bases, so the coherence witness was unreachable — only the truth table could be certified. Flight 2 moved the ebits to a physical relay outside the shield, and the same gate became fully witnessed.

THE WELD, REUSED AND COMPOSED

Every flight here reuses the campaign's own moves: the 197 relay's classical-bit weld (no feed-forward needed), and the in-block logical gates from 206/214. New content is the composition — a gate split across a cut, then proven quantum, then multiplied across two cuts into a network.

SCOPE & FENCE

Per-variant partial shield (one stabilizer type checked per basis, not both at once, as in 197/217). Physical relay ebits are a transient resource, not themselves shielded — the shield protects the data. Flight 3's GHZ certificate uses two Z-correlators plus the three-body ⟨X̄X̄X̄⟩ phase, which excludes separable/classical-mixture states at >100σ; a full Mermin-type witness would be stronger but was not flown.
