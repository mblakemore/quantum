GHZ metrology · beating the standard quantum limit

A sextant made of entanglement.

To measure a tiny phase, you watch an interference fringe. Tie N probes into one entangled GHZ state and the fringe oscillates N times faster — super-resolution — carrying up to N² the sensitivity of N separate probes. Dial up the probes and watch the fringe sharpen and the advantage climb the Heisenberg ladder. Every value is measured on silicon.

GHZ phase super-resolution is textbook (Bollinger 1996). The contribution: the frozen, executed-reference measurement.

The GHZ fringe's N× frequency is what carries the advantage: sensitivity to a phase grows as the fringe frequency squared, so N entangled probes reach N² Fisher information against the N of separate probes — the Heisenberg scaling, measured against a separable reference we actually ran.

Exhibit B — The Heisenberg Ladder

The advantage climbs — and does not give up at N=5

Each rung is the measured Fisher advantage against the ideal Heisenberg line R = N. The advantage tracks just below the line and keeps climbing through N=5 — where a deeper task (capacity activation, F85) had already inverted. On this hardware, cheap-prep metrology stays in the quantum regime.
