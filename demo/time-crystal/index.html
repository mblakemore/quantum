The Time Crystal.

Kick a disordered chain of qubits over and over with a flip that's deliberately wrong by 12%. On its own, the wobble it makes drifts and cancels itself out — beats to a standstill. Switch on the interactions between the qubits and the rhythm locks: it keeps ticking at exactly half the drive, refusing to drift, immune to the error you dialed in. A phase of matter whose order is in time — a clock nothing set.

Scope: signature, not a thermodynamic-limit proof — 6 qubits, finite coherence, one disorder realization. The claim lives at the beat-null, where the plain chain dies and the crystal doesn't.

The court

A discrete time crystal breaks time-translation symmetry: driven at period T, it responds at period 2T, and — the part that makes it a phase, not a fluke — that response is rigid. Detune the drive by 12% and a plain chain's rhythm drifts and self-cancels; the crystal's does not, because interactions plus strong disorder localize the system and lock the subharmonic. On ibm_fez, at the very period where the plain chain beats to a standstill, the crystal is still ticking at 0.78.

FALSIFIABLE

Turn the interactions off in the crystal arm and it must reproduce the plain chain — the crystal is the interactions, not the pulse. Verified noiseless before flight; the test can fail.

BETTER THAN PREDICTED

The tick strength decayed only 0.996→0.376 over 120 two-qubit gates, where the noise model predicted ≈0.12 — the localization that rigidifies the rhythm also shields it from noise. The phase is partly self-protecting.

HONEST FENCE

The control has no two-qubit gates, so it barely decoheres and its beat revives at late periods; the clean claim is the first beat-null. A gate-matched control (a non-localizing interacting chain) is the next rung.
