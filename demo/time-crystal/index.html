The Time Crystal.

Kick a disordered chain of qubits over and over with a flip that's deliberately wrong by 12%. On its own, the wobble it makes drifts and cancels itself out — beats to a standstill. Switch on the interactions between the qubits and the rhythm locks: it keeps ticking at exactly half the drive, refusing to drift, immune to the error you dialed in. A phase of matter whose order is in time — a clock nothing set.

Scope: signature, not a thermodynamic-limit proof — 6 qubits, finite coherence, one disorder realization. The claim lives at the beat-null, where the plain chain dies and the crystal doesn't.

2 · The melting point — and disorder doesn't help

ibm_fez · sweep the drive error ε and the disorder strength W · where does the rhythm die?

Push the drive error ε up and eventually even the crystal melts. The natural guess — the one textbooks suggest — is that disorder should protect the crystal, letting it survive to larger ε. The hardware says the opposite: more disorder melts it sooner. The clean interaction-only chain (W=0) survives to ε≈0.33; strong disorder collapses it at ε≈0.19. Disorder spreads the rhythm across sites and weakens the signal — it gives the crystal its rigidity, but it is not the thing holding the melting point back.
rigid (rhythm locked) melted melt boundary ε_melt

And the melt boundary matches the noiseless prediction to ~0.007 in ε at every W — noise dims the signal but does not move the boundary. So disorder is a bystander to noise too: interactions set both the rigidity and the noise protection; disorder only lowers the amplitude and shrinks the boundary.

The court

A discrete time crystal breaks time-translation symmetry: driven at period T, it responds at period 2T, and — the part that makes it a phase, not a fluke — that response is rigid. Detune the drive by 12% and a plain chain's rhythm drifts and self-cancels; the crystal's does not, because interactions plus strong disorder localize the system and lock the subharmonic. On ibm_fez, at the very period where the plain chain beats to a standstill, the crystal is still ticking at 0.78.

FALSIFIABLE

Turn the interactions off in the crystal arm and it must reproduce the plain chain — the crystal is the interactions, not the pulse. Verified noiseless before flight; the test can fail.

CORRECTED · NO ANOMALOUS SHIELD

We first reported the decay (0.996→0.376 over 120 gates) as a ~2.9× "noise shield" beyond prediction. That 2.9× was a baseline mismatch: the prediction borrowed another qubit set's gate error (0.0106); these qubits measure 0.0021, and against their own baseline the crystal decays exactly at the generic gate-error rate — ratio 1.00, no anomalous protection (Exp151b-v2). The rigidity results here are same-qubit comparisons and stand untouched. The record self-corrected in three cycles — that process is part of the exhibit.

MECHANISM CONFIRMED

The plain chain's whole curve — through the null to −0.98 at period 8 and reviving — tracks the parameter-free beat cos(πεt) to an RMS of 0.009 (≤1.5%). The crystal is monotonic, no beat. A 13-point shape discrimination; the late revival is the beat doing exactly what theory demands. Fence: finite chain, one disorder realization — a signature, not a limit proof.
