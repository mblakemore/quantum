The Sealed Shadow

Two copies of a quantum state, measured together, read a sealed secret that one copy at a time provably cannot — using 9.3× fewer copies than any single-copy strategy can achieve, proven blind, on real hardware.

The separation

A weight-12 Pauli was sealed before the flight, hidden among 416 ≈ 4.3 billion possibilities. Two-copy Bell sampling recovered it. The tall bar is the sealed operator; every other bar is a wrong Pauli, measured through the same pipeline.

The two probe families are not the same thing, and the chart does not pretend they are. The 64 weight-heavy probes are true shot noise — largest 0.0069, 2.3σ. The 48 weight-1 probes carry signed deviations up to 0.0403 at 13.6σ, which the grader attributes to cross-copy correlation in the transversal Bell block and records as a registered limitation rather than noise. A negative tr² estimate is an impossible output for any single-copy state property, so the sign itself is the detector. It is 20–25× below the failed flight’s signature and does not touch the planted result.

The floor holds against ADAPTIVE strategies, not just simple ones. Confirmed from the primary source by the grading seat, by independent extraction of arXiv:2404.19105: the single-copy floor is proven in the adaptive learning-tree model — Definition 1, verbatim, “The choice of POVM can depend on all previous measurement outcomes”. So the 9.3× stands over any single-copy strategy, adaptive included. (Definitions 1 and 6 — not the paper’s introductory overview text.)

Scope. Single instance, one die, one epoch. The advantage is in copies of ρ consumed only — classical post-processing is Θ(4n) on both arms and is excluded from the claim. Not a runtime or total-work advantage. Not sign-learning. Not a below-threshold fault-tolerance result.

The advantage, as copies spent

Both learners chase the same hidden Pauli. The two-copy learner finishes; the single-copy learner cannot finish at this budget — not because it is slow, but because a theorem says so.

We report the smallest number in the chain, not the largest. Nominal 14.4× → sizing 14.1× → delivered 9.3×. The claim evaluates at delivered amplitude over copies actually spent. The single-copy lane never completes here because completing it would misstate the theorem.

Flight 1 — FAILED AS FROZEN

A one-line prep bug left seven qubits un-randomized. The blind weight-1 tripwires localized it to the exact qubits, from sealed data, before anyone could see the answer.

The fix, validated from the blind side

The decode seat — unable to see the answer — reported the failure signature absent on identity positions it could not know. The unseal proved it right.

Flight 2 — the re-fly

A fresh seal bound to a single-use authorization, sized mid-air to the epoch it actually occupied, landing in 109 seconds under budget.

A museum that shows only wins teaches that wins are easy. This exhibit’s wing also holds a decoder race that this campaign won and then retired by its own red team.
