The event that never happens.

Three joint outcomes were pinned near zero. If the two qubits carry definite instructions — any story where each already knows what it will answer — then those three near-zeros force a fourth outcome to be no bigger than their sum. It is not an inequality with a tuned bound and it needs no optimisation: it is counting. The fourth outcome happened 8.7% of the time.

The bound comes out of the same data

Nothing cited, nothing tuned — the three measured zeros ARE the bound

The local-realist ceiling on the fourth event is the sum of the other three. Because the three zeros came in imperfect, the ceiling they set is higher than an idealised one — which makes the test harder, not easier. It cleared anyway.

The three zeros sum to 2.92%. That is the whole allowance every local-realist description has for the fourth event. It arrived at 8.69%. The difference — W = +0.0576 ± 0.0037 — is 15.7σ of daylight, and the measured Hardy fraction landed at 96% of the ideal maximum the effect allows.
