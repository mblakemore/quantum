The Subspace Channel.

Now that the network can entangle two strangers, here is what it's for. Alice and Bob — who never interacted — share a swapped pair, turn it into a secret key, and check with a Bell test that no one else holds a copy. They one-time-pad a message: BEAM ME UP. Then a wiretap tries to listen — and the same Bell test that certifies the key catches the tap and burns it down. A channel you cannot eavesdrop without announcing yourself. Scope: entanglement-based QKD (E91/BBM92) over the swapped pair, with the CHSH certificate and one intercept-resend attack — a physics-certified-key demonstration, not a deployed cryptosystem.

The court

why the key is trustworthy, why the tap can't hide, and the fine print

THE KEY IS CERTIFIED, NOT ASSUMED

S = 2.179 beats the classical limit of 2 at 6σ — the shared randomness is genuine entanglement, which (by monogamy) no third party can hold a copy of. The security rests on a measured Bell violation, not on trusting the hardware. The message round-tripped: "BEAM ME UP" → cipher → "BEAM ME UP", 100% correct

THE WIRETAP CANNOT HIDE

An intercept-resend Eve on Bob's qubit collapses the certificate (S 2.179 → 1.184) and drives the X-basis error rate to 50.7% — a coin flip. The protocol sees it and aborts the key. The falsifier passing is the security feature: you cannot listen without announcing yourself.

WHY TWO BASES — SHOWN, NOT ASSERTED

Eve listens in Z, so the Z-basis error stays clean (8%) while the X-basis error explodes (51%). A protocol checking one basis would have missed her completely. This is exactly why BB84/E91 use conjugate bases — here it's in the data, not the textbook.

THE FINE PRINT — AND A DEAD END

The secret fraction is thin (0.04): swap-grade fidelity barely clears the key threshold. The obvious fix — run purification first — we tried, and it did not pay: two bracketing flights (Exp167) showed single-round distillation is underwater on this hardware — its two-qubit-gate overhead costs more fidelity than it recovers, at every input we could reach. The fatter key here awaits better gates or a lower-overhead protocol, not a free win from the stack. One die, co-located parties, one attack, raw key (no privacy amplification).
